# About this book

_Principles of Computer Music_ is a free online textbook that teaches the technical principles and creative practice of musical computing from a computer science perspective in Python.

This textbook was written by [Chris Donahue](https://chrisdonahue.com) as a companion to CMU's Intro to Computer Music Course (15-322/622).

© 2026 Chris Donahue. All rights reserved. **Do not copy or redistribute without permission**. See [LICENSE](license.md) for details.

## Organization

This text is roughly organized into two parts:

- **Part 1: _Foundations_** (Chapters 0-10): Foundational material for computer music.
- **Part 2: _Topics_** (Chapters 11-19): Specific topics that build upon the foundation.

## Status

**This text is a _work in progress_**. It is being updated regularly during the first offering of the revamped course in Fall 2026. Afterwards, the completed text will be stamped as the first edition.

## AI disclosure

:::{important}
**AI Disclosure**: To allow for a full supporting text to be written on a short summer timeline, this text was written with heavy support from AI. Every word has been thoroughly reviewed by the author (Chris Donahue).
:::

This book was written with support from AI, specifically Claude Opus 4.6 through 5. At a high level, most of the technical content and organization were written by Chris, while most of the prose and figure code were generated by Claude. The authorship process for each chapter was as follows:

1. Chris wrote a detailed outline, specifying all of the content and organization
1. Chris added his lecture slides and code from previous years of the course as additional references
1. Claude was instructed to generate a chapter draft based on the outline, slides, and `AGENTS.md` file
1. Chris read every word of the chapter draft and made manual changes and/or left feedback
1. Claude was asked to address any feedback
1. This iterative process continued until Chris was satisfied with the chapter

For transparency, the original handwritten outlines for each chapter are preserved in the [textbook's GitHub repo](https://github.com/chrisdonahue/pcm), and the `git` history was carefully managed so that each change of agency between Chris/Claude is codified with a commit.
